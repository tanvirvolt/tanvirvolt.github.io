Formula for induction motor slip

Synchronous speed is calculated as Ns = 120 × f / P, where f is supply frequency in hertz and P is the number of stator poles. Slip speed is Ns − Nr, where Nr is the actual rotor speed in RPM. Percentage slip is s = ((Ns − Nr) / Ns) × 100%.

Worked example: 4-pole motor at 50 Hz

For a 4-pole motor supplied at 50 Hz, synchronous speed is 120 × 50 / 4 = 1500 RPM. If measured rotor speed is 1440 RPM, slip speed is 60 RPM and slip is (60 / 1500) × 100 = 4%.

How to interpret the result

  • In normal motoring operation, rotor speed is typically below synchronous speed, so slip is positive.
  • Slip changes with load, supply conditions, motor design and operating state; compare readings under similar conditions.
  • A result at or above synchronous speed can indicate a generating/braking condition or incorrect input; check the operating context before interpreting it.
Engineering note: This calculator provides a basic speed/slip calculation. It does not diagnose motor condition, estimate efficiency, or replace manufacturer data and safe maintenance procedures.
